problem: |
  Respiratory diseases such as asthma, bronchitis, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease affect millions of people worldwide and remain a major public health challenge. In urban areas like Baltimore, environmental       conditions such as air pollution, traffic congestion, temperature, humidity, and population density can increase the risk of respiratory illness. Communities living in areas with poor air quality are often affected the    most, which can contribute to health disparities and reduced quality of life. 

  This project explores how artificial intelligence and machine learning can be used to better predict respiratory health risks using environmental data. Current approaches often struggle to efficiently analyze large and    complex datasets or identify patterns that may help with early risk detection. Our project aims to address this gap by developing and comparing machine learning models such as Support Vector Machines, Random Forest, and   Extreme Learning Machines, while also improving prediction performance using optimization techniques. 

  The goal of this research is to support more accurate and data driven approaches to environmental health analysis. By improving how respiratory risks are predicted, this work could contribute to better public health       monitoring, earlier interventions, and more informed decision making in communities affected by environmental health challenges.
