project_title: "Predicting Respiratory Health Risks Using AI and Environmental Data"

problem: |
  Respiratory diseases such as asthma, bronchitis, and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)
  affect millions of people worldwide and remain a major public health concern. In urban areas like
  Baltimore, environmental conditions such as air pollution, traffic congestion, humidity,
  temperature, and population density can increase the risk of respiratory illness. Communities
  exposed to poor environmental conditions are often affected the most, contributing to health
  disparities and reduced quality of life.

  This project explores how artificial intelligence and machine learning can be used to better predict
  respiratory health risks using environmental and demographic data. Current approaches often
  struggle to efficiently analyze large and complex datasets or identify patterns that support early
  risk detection. Our research aims to address this gap by developing and comparing machine
  learning models such as Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest, and Extreme Learning
  Machines (ELM), while improving prediction performance through optimization techniques.

  The goal of this research is to support more accurate, data driven approaches to environmental
  health analysis. By improving respiratory risk prediction, this work could contribute to better
  public health monitoring, earlier intervention strategies, and more informed decision making in
  communities affected by environmental health challenges.

approach: |
  Our project follows a structured machine learning and optimization workflow:

  - Collect, clean, and preprocess environmental and demographic datasets related to respiratory health risks.
  - Perform exploratory data analysis and visualization to identify patterns and relationships within the data.
  - Implement machine learning models including Support Vector Machines (SVM), Random Forest, and Extreme Learning Machines (ELM).
  - Apply preprocessing techniques such as feature scaling, normalization, and class balancing to improve model performance.
  - Use the Douglas optimization algorithm to improve the efficiency and predictive accuracy of the SVM model.
  - Compare the performance of optimized and non-optimized models using evaluation metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall, RMSE, and F1-score.
  - Visualize and communicate findings through reports, charts, presentations, and research discussions.

  The primary tools used throughout the project include Python, Jupyter Notebook, MATLAB, R, and SPSS for machine learning implementation, statistical analysis, optimization, and data visualization.

outcome: |
  By the end of the program, I expect to contribute to the development of an AI driven
  machine learning workflow capable of predicting respiratory disease risks using environmental
  data. The project outcomes will include trained machine learning models, data visualizations,
  performance analysis reports, and a final research presentation.

  I also hope to strengthen my technical skills in artificial intelligence, optimization methods,
  data analysis, and research communication. Ultimately, this project demonstrates how AI and
  computational methods can be used to address real world environmental and public health
  challenges.
