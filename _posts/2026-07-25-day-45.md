what_i_learned: |
  Today marked the first day of the symposium, and it was filled with opportunities to learn, collaborate, and think creatively. We began the day by attending the opening keynote presented by Dr. Juan E. Gilbert, who spoke about creating fair and effective admissions platforms. He introduced Application Quest, a platform designed to improve the admissions process by making it more equitable, transparent, and accessible. His talk reinforced the importance of designing technology that not only solves problems but also promotes fairness and equal opportunity.
  
  After the keynote, I decided to participate in the symposium hackathon. The challenge focused on developing equitable, real-world AI solutions for K–12 education, with possible application areas including healthcare, education, agriculture, and civic engagement. My team spent the majority of the day brainstorming ideas, identifying real-world problems, and discussing how artificial intelligence could be used to create meaningful and practical solutions. It was exciting to collaborate with people who approached problems from different perspectives, and the brainstorming process reminded me that the strongest ideas often come from combining diverse experiences and viewpoints.
  
  We concluded the day by attending the final keynote presented by Jeffrey Bardzell, who discussed the consequences of irresponsible artificial intelligence and reflected on the ethical implications of AI in the context of the bombing of children in Iran. His presentation emphasized that technology is never neutral and that developers, researchers, and policymakers all share the responsibility of ensuring AI is designed and used ethically. It was a powerful reminder that while AI has tremendous potential to improve lives, it can also contribute to harm when developed or deployed without careful consideration of its societal impact.
  
blockers: |
  The biggest challenge today was balancing the excitement of the symposium with the fast pace of the hackathon. With so many ideas being discussed, it took time for our team to narrow our focus and decide on a solution that was both impactful and achievable within the limited timeframe.
  
reflection: |
  Today broadened my perspective on what it means to build responsible technology. From learning about fairness in admissions systems to discussing ethical AI and collaborating on a real-world hackathon challenge, I was reminded that technical skills alone are not enough. Great technology should be innovative, inclusive, and designed with people at the center. I'm excited to continue developing our hackathon project and see how our ideas evolve over the next few days.
