what_i_learned: |
  Today was a productive day focused on refining my dataset and strengthening the foundation of my research project. I finalized my feature selection for Chronic Heart Disease (CHD) prediction and expanded my analysis to include additional health-related factors such as asthma, diabetes, and mental health because of their known relationships with cardiovascular health. I also incorporated race-related variables to better understand demographic influences on CHD risk.
  
  After preparing the dataset, I conducted correlation analysis to explore how different environmental, demographic, socioeconomic, and health-related factors relate to CHD risk. Through this process, I learned that health and socioeconomic indicators appeared to have stronger relationships with CHD than many of the environmental variables I initially expected to be significant. I also gained a deeper understanding of how multicollinearity can affect predictive modeling and why feature selection is an important step before building machine learning models.
  
blockers: |
  I did not encounter any major blockers today. The most challenging part was carefully deciding which features should remain in the dataset and which ones might introduce redundancy due to high correlations with other variables. This required reviewing both the data and supporting research literature to ensure the selected features were meaningful and aligned with the goals of the project.
  
reflection: |
  Today's work felt like an important milestone because I moved beyond simply cleaning and exploring the data and began uncovering meaningful patterns within it. One of the most interesting findings was seeing how strongly factors such as diabetes, disability, asthma, and educational attainment were connected to CHD risk, while many environmental factors showed weaker direct relationships than anticipated. These results reminded me that health outcomes are often shaped by a complex combination of social, economic, and medical factors rather than a single cause.
  
  In the afternoon, our team shifted focus toward preparing for tomorrow's weekly showcase and Monday's Mid-Summer Symposium. We spent time designing slides, organizing content, dividing presentation responsibilities, and conducting a practice run. Seeing everyone's contributions come together made the project feel more complete (though we still have a lot more to do) and helped me better understand how my individual work fits into the larger research effort. The slides are coming together well, and I am excited to share our progress and continue building momentum as we move into the next phase of the project.
