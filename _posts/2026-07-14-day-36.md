what_i_learned: |
  Today was focused on refining my research paper and strengthening the results section of my machine learning framework. One of the biggest things I worked on was incorporating the Douglas-Rachford Support Vector Machine (DR-SVM) optimization approach into my research findings. This step was important because the project moved beyond simply comparing traditional machine learning models and explored how mathematical optimization techniques can improve predictive performance.
  
  Through this process, I learned that building a strong machine learning model is not only about selecting a powerful algorithm. The foundation begins with understanding the data, identifying meaningful patterns, addressing challenges such as class imbalance, and making intentional decisions throughout the modeling process.
  
  Working with the Environmental Justice Index dataset has shown me how deeply connected environmental, socioeconomic, and health factors are. Variables such as diabetes, disability, asthma, and educational attainment revealed important relationships with chronic disease risk. These patterns reminded me that machine learning can be used not only for prediction but also as a tool for understanding the factors affecting communities.
  
  I also refined the discussion around the optimized DR-SVM model. After tuning key parameters including the regularization parameter, L1 regularization coefficient, relaxation parameter, and threshold value, the model demonstrated improved performance. The optimized model achieved approximately 97.87% accuracy, 100% recall for the high-risk class, an F1-score of 97.92%, and an AUC above 0.99 during training. On unseen test data, it maintained strong generalization with an AUC of 0.924.
  
  Seeing these results was exciting because every metric represented more than just a number. It represented the process of testing, debugging, learning, and improving. Each adjustment made throughout the project contributed to building a stronger and more reliable framework.
  
blockers: |
  One of the challenges I faced today was ensuring that the results were presented clearly and accurately. Translating technical findings into a research paper requires more than reporting numbers. It requires explaining why those numbers matter and how they contribute to the overall purpose of the study.
  
  Another challenge was balancing the excitement of strong model performance with the responsibility of interpreting the results carefully. A model achieving high performance is important, but understanding its limitations, generalizability, and future applications is equally important.
  
  Research often requires revisiting previous sections, making adjustments, and ensuring that every part of the story connects. Although this process can be time-consuming, it continues to improve my ability to think critically about my work.
