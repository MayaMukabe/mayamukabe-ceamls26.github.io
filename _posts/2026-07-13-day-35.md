what_i_learned: |
  Today was a productive research day focused on strengthening both my understanding of the project and the quality of my research paper. I continued documenting my work in Overleaf by reporting the findings from the Cost-Sensitive Learning experiments and began developing the methodology section of my paper. Writing these sections helped me better understand how each stage of the machine learning pipeline contributes to producing reliable and reproducible results.
  
  I also spent time researching the economic burden of respiratory and chronic diseases in Maryland. Learning about the millions of dollars spent annually on conditions such as asthma and the billions associated with diabetes provided important real-world context for my research. It reinforced why developing accurate predictive models for chronic diseases is valuable, not only from a technical perspective but also for supporting public health planning and resource allocation.
  
  As I continued refining my paper, I reviewed and documented the overall system workflow, including the Environmental Justice Index (EJI) dataset, the data preprocessing pipeline, multicollinearity analysis using Variance Inflation Factor (VIF), class imbalance strategies such as SMOTE, Cost-Sensitive Learning and class weighting, and the evaluation framework using stratified train-test splitting. I also summarized the three machine learning algorithms evaluated in this study: Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), and Extreme Learning Machine (ELM).
  
  Toward the end of the day, our mentor, Ms. Amara, introduced us to the Douglas Optimization Algorithm, which we will use tomorrow to optimize our best-performing machine learning models. It was exciting to see how optimization techniques can further improve model performance beyond the initial training process.
  
blockers: |
  One of my biggest challenges today was balancing technical writing with continuing my literature and background research. Research papers require careful organization, and I wanted to make sure that every section flowed logically while accurately representing the work I have completed. I also found myself spending considerable time verifying statistics and ensuring that the background information I included was supported by reliable sources.
  
reflection: |
  Today reminded me that research is much more than building machine learning models. It involves understanding the problem, communicating the methodology clearly, and demonstrating why the work matters. Looking into the economic impact of chronic diseases helped me connect my technical work with real-world healthcare challenges, making the purpose of the project even more meaningful.
  
  I am also looking forward to tomorrow as we begin applying the Douglas Optimization Algorithm to our best-performing models. It feels rewarding to see the project progressing from data preparation and model evaluation toward optimization, and I am excited to see how much further we can improve our predictive performance.
