what_i_learned: |
  Today's sessions focused heavily on understanding research literature and strengthening our ability to analyze academic papers. During the literature review workshop, I learned that research is an iterative process. We research to write, and we write to research. A few key takeaways were the importance of creating concise and attention-grabbing titles, writing with clarity, and critically evaluating sources before deciding whether they should be included in a study.
  In our first research lab session, each team member took turns explaining sections of code to ensure that we truly understood what the code was doing rather than simply running it. Our graduate mentor, Ms. Amara, also divided us into groups and assigned research papers for analysis. This helped us begin thinking more critically about research methodologies and how they connect to our own project.
  During the second research lab session, we worked on our weekly report presentation and completed a mini Python project. We also analyzed a research paper focused on predicting respiratory disease cases using machine learning. The study was inspired by COVID-19 and addressed a gap in previous research by combining climate and air-pollution factors to improve disease prediction. The researchers used data from South Korea and applied machine learning methods such as Gradient Boosting, Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), and SHAP to better understand the factors influencing respiratory disease occurrence.
  
blockers: |
  I did not encounter any major blockers today. However, reading and analyzing research papers requires careful attention to detail, especially when identifying research gaps and understanding methodology choices.

reflection: |
  Today helped me see research from a different perspective. Before, I mainly focused on the results of a study, but now I am paying closer attention to how researchers identify problems, justify their methods, and communicate their findings. Analyzing research papers also gave me a clearer picture of how our own project fits into the broader field of machine learning and respiratory health prediction. One thing that stood out to me was the importance of asking the right question. A strong research project is not just about finding answers. It starts with identifying a meaningful gap and understanding why that gap matters. That insight will be valuable as our team continues developing our own research study.
