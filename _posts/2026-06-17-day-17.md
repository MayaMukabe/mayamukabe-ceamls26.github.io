what_i_learned: |
  Today started with everyone taking turns presenting what we have been learning in R. My presentation focused on performing operations in R, installing packages, and some of the key differences between R and Python. I also did a live demonstration to show how these concepts work in practice. Listening to everyone else's presentations helped me learn new ways of plotting graphs, reading visualizations, and finding correlations within datasets.
  Afterward, we finished reading the research papers assigned to us. I presented my findings on a study that used machine learning to predict respiratory disease occurrence in Seoul, South Korea. The study combined climate and air pollution factors such as temperature, humidity, PM2.5, and carbon monoxide to predict respiratory illnesses. The researchers used Gradient Boosting, Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), and SHAP analysis to identify the most influential environmental factors affecting respiratory health.
  One key takeaway from the paper was that lower temperatures, lower humidity, and higher levels of certain pollutants were strongly associated with increased respiratory disease cases. I also learned how researchers preprocess data, perform feature selection, evaluate model performance, and interpret machine learning predictions.
  After presenting the paper, I began searching for datasets that align with our research project and contain similar environmental and health-related features that we can use for model development.
  
blockers: |
  I did not face any major blockers today. The biggest challenge was understanding some of the technical details within the research paper, but presenting the study helped strengthen my understanding of the methodology and results.
  
reflection: |
  Today felt like a bridge between learning concepts and applying them to real research. Presenting both my R programming knowledge and my research paper findings gave me a chance to practice explaining technical information in a clear way. It was also interesting to see how the ideas from the paper connect directly to our project. As we continue moving forward, I am becoming more comfortable reading research papers, analyzing methodologies, and thinking about how data can be used to solve real-world problems. Finding datasets that match our research needs made the project feel even more tangible, and I am excited to see what next week brings as we begin working on different models like the Support Vector Machine and the Extreme Learning Machine.
