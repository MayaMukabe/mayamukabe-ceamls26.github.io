what_i_learned: |
  Today I continued documenting my research by populating the Overleaf template with the Introduction and Dataset Description sections of my paper. This helped me organize my work into a formal research paper while clearly explaining the motivation behind the project and the data preparation process.
  
  Afterward, I focused on understanding the complete machine learning pipeline for predicting Chronic Heart Disease (CHD). I learned how to separate the dataset into feature variables and the target variable before splitting the data into training and testing sets. I also learned why feature scaling is necessary, especially for models like Support Vector Machines (SVM), since variables in the dataset are measured on different scales.
  
  A major topic I explored today was class imbalance. Since only about 20% of the dataset represents high CHD risk, I learned how SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) creates synthetic samples of the minority class to produce a balanced training dataset. I also learned why SMOTE should only be applied to the training data to prevent data leakage and ensure an unbiased evaluation.
  
  In addition, I studied how different machine learning models are trained and evaluated. I learned how Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest, and Extreme Learning Machine (ELM) are trained, how GridSearchCV is used to identify the best hyperparameters, and how performance is measured using metrics such as Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, ROC-AUC, MAE, and RMSE. Finally, I learned how visualizations such as ROC curves and performance heatmaps help compare model performance and make the results easier to interpret.
  
blockers: |
  Today's material was more advanced than previous topics, particularly understanding how SMOTE generates synthetic data and how hyperparameter tuning with GridSearchCV improves model performance. It took extra time to understand the reasoning behind each step instead of simply following the code, but working through the concepts helped strengthen my understanding.
  
reflection: |
  Today marked an important step in connecting theory with practice. Instead of only preparing data, I began understanding how each stage of the machine learning pipeline contributes to building reliable predictive models. I now have a much clearer understanding of why data preprocessing, balancing techniques, model optimization, and evaluation metrics are essential before trusting a model's predictions. It was rewarding to see how all the concepts I've learned over the past few weeks are beginning to fit together into one complete workflow. I'm looking forward to continuing with model development and comparing the performance of the different algorithms on my CHD dataset.
