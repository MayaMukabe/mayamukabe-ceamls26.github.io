what_i_learned: |
  Today was all about bringing the project closer to completion. I spent most of my time refining the visualizations that summarize the performance of all the machine learning models developed throughout this research. I created publication-quality charts comparing the Support Vector Machine (SVM), Random Forest (RF), and Extreme Learning Machine (ELM) models across every phase of the project, including training on balanced data, Stratified K-Fold cross-validation, and the Douglas-Rachford optimized SVM.
  
  The visualizations present important evaluation metrics such as Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score, ROC-AUC, RMSE, MAE, and RMSE Percentage Difference. Seeing all of the results side by side made it much easier to understand how each model performed under different training strategies and optimization techniques. It also helped identify the strengths of each approach and reinforced why the optimized Douglas-Rachford SVM emerged as one of the strongest models in the study.
  
  Beyond creating the figures, I continued refining the research paper by ensuring that the reported results, discussions, and conclusions accurately reflected the experimental findings. It was rewarding to see weeks of data preprocessing, feature engineering, model development, cross-validation, balancing techniques, optimization, and evaluation finally come together into one complete story.
  
blockers: |
  My biggest challenge today was making sure the visualizations communicated the results clearly without overwhelming the reader. Choosing the right metrics, organizing the comparisons, and presenting everything in a consistent format required several revisions. I also had to be careful that every figure aligned with the narrative in the research paper.
  
reflection: |
  Today reminded me that research is not only about building accurate models but also about communicating results effectively. A well-designed visualization can explain what pages of text sometimes cannot. Looking back, it is amazing to see how much this project has evolved, from cleaning a large environmental dataset to developing and optimizing multiple machine learning models for respiratory health risk prediction. With the symposium approaching, I feel confident that the work is coming together, and I am excited to share everything our team has accomplished over the past several weeks.
