what_i_learned: |
  Today I focused on strengthening the way I communicate my research by incorporating the feedback my mentor provided on reporting the results of the Douglas-optimized Support Vector Machine (SVM). I learned that presenting research is not just about reporting metrics, but also about clearly explaining what the results mean and why they matter.
  
  I also attended an insightful session by Dr. Chris Crawford on Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs). I learned how BCIs capture brain signals, such as electrical activity and blood oxygen levels, to enable communication between the brain and external systems. The session explored applications of BCIs in clinical medicine, gaming, user-state monitoring, and brain-robot interaction. One of the most interesting takeaways was the importance of building modular Brain-Robot Interaction (BRI) systems using standardized frameworks like the Robotics Operating System (ROS), making these technologies more scalable and accessible as BCI devices become increasingly affordable.
  
  Another highlight of the day was watching everyone's project pitch videos. I enjoyed seeing the creativity each team brought to presenting their research. The commercial-style approach made complex projects engaging and showed me the value of storytelling when communicating technical work.
  
blockers: |
  Today's biggest challenge was refining the research paper to clearly communicate the significance of my findings. Incorporating feedback required me to think critically about my explanations rather than simply presenting results. It took several revisions to ensure the discussion accurately reflected the purpose and impact of the Douglas optimization model.
  
reflection: |
  Today reminded me that good research is only as impactful as the way it is communicated. While developing strong models is important, explaining the reasoning, significance, and real-world implications behind the results is equally valuable. The BCI session also broadened my perspective on how artificial intelligence extends beyond traditional machine learning into fields like neuroscience and robotics. I ended the day continuing to refine my research paper with a greater appreciation for both technical excellence and effective scientific communication.
