what_i_learned: |
  Today I focused on preparing my dataset for machine learning by exploring different ways to handle the class imbalance in my CHD data. Instead of relying on only one technique, I looked into cost-sensitive learning and class weighting methods to give more importance to the minority class during model training. This helped me understand that balancing data is not always about creating new samples; sometimes the model itself can be adjusted to pay more attention to underrepresented cases.
  
  I also spent time learning the theory behind Support Vector Machines (SVM) and Extreme Learning Machines (ELM). I watched videos and studied how these models work, why they are used, and what makes them different. For SVM, I learned that the goal is to find the best boundary that separates classes while maximizing the margin between them. For ELM, I learned that it is a fast neural-network-based approach that can train efficiently by randomly assigning hidden-layer weights and solving the output weights mathematically.
  
blockers: |
  My main challenge today was deciding which balancing strategy might be the most appropriate for my CHD dataset. Since there are multiple approaches, I spent time comparing their advantages and limitations instead of immediately choosing one.
  
reflection: |
  Today felt less like coding and more like building a deeper understanding of the machine learning process. I realized that choosing the right model is important, but preparing the data and understanding why a model works can be just as important. Learning about SVM and ELM helped me connect the mathematical ideas behind machine learning to the real problem I am trying to solve. I am excited to start testing these models on my dataset and see how the different balancing methods affect their performance.
