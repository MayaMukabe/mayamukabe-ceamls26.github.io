what_i_learned: |
  Today was a full day in the research lab, and I spent most of my time preparing and exploring the dataset for our machine learning project. My focus area is Coronary Heart Disease (CHD), and I learned several important steps involved in preparing data before building predictive models.
  
  I started by selecting the environmental, demographic, socioeconomic, and health-related variables that are most relevant to predicting CHD risk in Baltimore. After creating a dedicated CHD dataset, I cleaned the data by identifying missing values that had been recorded as -999. I replaced these placeholders with proper missing values and then filled them using the median of each feature to ensure the dataset remained reliable and ready for analysis.
  
  I also learned how to use visualizations to better understand data. Histograms helped me examine the distribution of each feature, while boxplots allowed me to identify potential outliers. Some variables showed relatively balanced distributions, while others were heavily skewed or contained extreme values. Finally, I used the Interquartile Range (IQR) method to calculate and quantify outliers across all features. This gave me a clearer picture of which variables may require additional attention before model development.
  
  At the end of the day, each team member presented their findings. Since different members are focusing on CHD, asthma, diabetes, and cancer, listening to everyone's presentations helped me better understand how environmental and social factors influence various health outcomes and how our individual projects connect to the broader goal of predicting health risks.
  
blockers: |
  I did not encounter any major blockers today. The data cleaning and visualization process went smoothly, and I was able to follow the workflow without significant issues. The main challenge was interpreting some of the highly skewed distributions and understanding why certain variables contained many outliers. However, discussing findings with my teammates helped provide additional context and made the results easier to understand.
  
reflection: |
  Today felt like an important step forward in my research journey. Rather than simply looking at numbers, I began to see the story hidden inside the data. Every histogram, boxplot, and outlier count revealed something unique about the communities represented in the dataset and the factors that may contribute to heart disease risk.
  
  The presentations at the end of the day were especially valuable because they showed how our individual investigations fit together like pieces of a larger puzzle. Hearing about asthma, diabetes, and cancer alongside my CHD analysis helped me appreciate the broader impact of environmental and social determinants on public health. I am excited to continue exploring the data, uncover deeper patterns, and see how these insights translate into meaningful machine learning models in the coming weeks.
