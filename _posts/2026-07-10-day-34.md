what_i_learned: |
  Today I continued strengthening my machine learning workflow by exploring Cost-Sensitive Learning as an alternative technique for handling class imbalance. Unlike SMOTE, which creates synthetic minority class samples, Cost-Sensitive Learning adjusts the model by assigning a higher penalty to misclassifying the minority class. Learning the difference between these two approaches helped me understand that class imbalance can be addressed either at the data level or at the algorithm level.
  
  I also analyzed the results produced by the cost-sensitive models and began documenting my findings in my research paper. Reviewing the evaluation metrics, confusion matrices, and ROC curves gave me a clearer understanding of how different balancing strategies influence model performance and the ability to correctly identify high-risk CHD cases.
  
blockers: |
  My biggest challenge today was interpreting the model evaluation results beyond simply looking at which model had the highest accuracy. Understanding how Precision, Recall, F1-score, ROC-AUC, MAE, and RMSE collectively describe model performance required careful analysis. Since each metric highlights a different aspect of performance, deciding which results were most meaningful for my research took time.
  
reflection: |
  Today reminded me that building a good machine learning model is about much more than training algorithms. It requires understanding the reasoning behind every preprocessing step, balancing technique, evaluation metric, and visualization. As I continue documenting my research in Overleaf, I can see the project gradually coming together into a complete story rather than just a collection of code. Every experiment and every comparison brings me one step closer to developing a reliable framework for predicting coronary heart disease risk, and I am looking forward to comparing all of my modeling approaches to determine which performs best.
